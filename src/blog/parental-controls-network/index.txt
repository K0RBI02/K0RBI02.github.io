15:T10ff,<p>The first tablet, the first phone, the game console in the living room: at some point every family wonders how to set limits online. Parents often search for the one setting that solves it, only to find out that children are remarkably good at finding the gaps in any single measure.</p>
<p>The good news: combining a few layers works well, and none of them requires constant supervision.</p>
<h2 id="why-one-layer-is-not-enough">Why One Layer Is Not Enough</h2>
<p>Every control has a blind spot:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Router settings</strong> only apply at home. On mobile data or at a friend's place they do nothing.</li>
<li><strong>DNS filters</strong> can be bypassed by browsers with built‑in encrypted DNS or by VPN apps.</li>
<li><strong>Device settings</strong> are strong, but each device and platform has its own.</li>
</ul>
<p>Used together, the weaknesses of one layer are covered by the others.</p>
<h2 id="layer-1-the-router">Layer 1: The Router</h2>
<p>Most routers can group devices into <strong>profiles</strong> and apply rules per profile:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Online times:</strong> for example no internet after 9 pm on school days.</li>
<li><strong>Time budgets:</strong> a daily allowance per device.</li>
<li><strong>Filter lists:</strong> block categories of websites, depending on the router.</li>
</ul>
<p>Assign each child's devices to their own profile. Name devices clearly in the router's device list, so you know which tablet is whose. Make sure the router's admin password is not known to the children, which is one of the points in our <a href="/src/blog/router-hardening/">router checklist</a>.</p>
<h2 id="layer-2-familyfriendly-dns">Layer 2: Family‑Friendly DNS</h2>
<p>A filtering DNS service blocks adult content, malware and phishing for every device that uses it:</p>
<div class="table-wrap"><table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Option</th>
<th>What it blocks</th>
<th>Verdict</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Cloudflare for Families (<code>1.1.1.3</code>)</td>
<td>Malware and adult content</td>
<td>Free and simple; no settings to adjust</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Hosted filters such as NextDNS</td>
<td>Categories, individual sites, per device</td>
<td>Very flexible, with schedules and reports</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Your own filter (Pi‑hole, AdGuard Home)</td>
<td>Whatever lists you choose</td>
<td>Full control, needs <a href="/src/blog/first-home-server/">a small home server</a></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table></div>
<p>Set the DNS on the router so it applies to the whole network, and, on devices that support it, enter the same service as <em>Private DNS</em> so it also works on mobile data. <a href="/src/blog/dns-explained/">DNS explained</a> covers the background.</p>
<h2 id="layer-3-the-devices-themselves">Layer 3: The Devices Themselves</h2>
<p>The operating systems have the most precise controls, and they travel with the device:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Android:</strong> <em>Google Family Link</em> for screen time, app approval and location.</li>
<li><strong>iPhone and iPad:</strong> <em>Screen Time</em> with Family Sharing, including "Ask to Buy".</li>
<li><strong>Game consoles:</strong> each platform has its own family settings, usually managed through a parent app.</li>
</ul>
<p>These settings also prevent the easiest bypasses, such as installing a VPN app or changing network settings.</p>
<h2 id="talking-beats-blocking">Talking Beats Blocking</h2>
<p>No filter is perfect, and older children will test every limit. The most effective protection is a conversation:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Explain the rules</strong> and agree on them together, including what happens when they are broken.</li>
<li><strong>Agree on what to do</strong> when something online feels wrong: tell a parent, without fear of losing the device.</li>
<li><strong>Loosen the rules with age.</strong> A ten‑year‑old needs different limits than a fifteen‑year‑old, and visible trust works better than secret monitoring.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>Parental controls work best in layers: time rules on the router, a family DNS filter for the whole network and mobile devices, and the built‑in controls of each operating system to close the gaps. Combine them with clear, shared rules and an open door for questions.
