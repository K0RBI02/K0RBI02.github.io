15:T1177,<p>For decades, the office phone was a separate world: its own wires, its own box on the wall, its own technician. That world is ending. Across Europe, providers have replaced or are replacing classic analogue and ISDN lines with telephony over the internet connection, and many businesses notice only when the old phone system stops working.</p>
<p>The good news: IP telephony is cheaper and more flexible than what came before, as long as the network underneath is ready for it.</p>
<h2 id="how-it-works">How It Works</h2>
<p>Calls travel as data packets over the internet connection, using a protocol called <strong>SIP</strong>. There are two common setups:</p>
<div class="table-wrap"><table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Setup</th>
<th>Where the phone system lives</th>
<th>Verdict</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Cloud phone system</td>
<td>At a provider; desk phones and apps connect to it over the internet</td>
<td>No hardware to maintain; the usual choice for small businesses</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Own phone system + SIP trunk</td>
<td>On site; the provider delivers lines via SIP</td>
<td>Sensible with many extensions or special requirements</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Collaboration platform</td>
<td>Phone numbers inside Teams, Webex or similar</td>
<td>Practical if everyone already works in that tool</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table></div>
<p>Phones can be desk phones, apps on smartphones and laptops (<em>softphones</em>), or both, with the same number ringing everywhere.</p>
<h2 id="what-it-needs-from-the-network">What It Needs From the Network</h2>
<p>A phone call needs little bandwidth, around 100 kbit/s per call, but it is sensitive to delay and jitter:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Low latency under load.</strong> If a cloud backup saturates the upload, calls start to break up. Smart queue management or prioritisation for voice solves this, see <a href="/blog/video-calls-bufferbloat/">why calls stutter</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Cables for desk phones.</strong> Most desk phones are powered by PoE from the switch and pass the network on to the computer behind them.</li>
<li><strong>No SIP ALG.</strong> Many routers have a "SIP helper" that rewrites call packets and often breaks them instead. Phone providers usually recommend switching it off.</li>
<li><strong>A separate network for voice</strong> is worth considering in larger offices, so phones are not affected by other traffic (<a href="/blog/segmenting-your-home-network/">segmenting networks</a>).</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="emergencies-and-power-cuts">Emergencies and Power Cuts</h2>
<p>This is where IP telephony differs most from the old landline:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>No power, no phone.</strong> The old phone line carried its own power. An IP phone, the switch and the router need electricity. A small <a href="/blog/ups-for-your-network/">UPS for the network corner</a> keeps phones working through short outages, and mobile phones remain the backup for longer ones. If mobile reception inside the building is weak, Wi‑Fi calling keeps those phones usable too.</li>
<li><strong>Emergency calls need the right address.</strong> Emergency services use the address registered for your number. Make sure it is correct for every site, and that staff working from home know that a softphone may report the office address.</li>
<li><strong>Alarm systems, lifts and fax machines</strong> that used the old line need to be checked. Some work over IP with an adapter, others need a mobile module.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="moving-without-losing-your-numbers">Moving Without Losing Your Numbers</h2>
<p>Your existing phone numbers can almost always be taken to a new provider (<em>number porting</em>). Plan it as a project:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>List everything that uses the phone line</strong>: extensions, fax, alarms, card terminals, lift emergency phones.</li>
<li><strong>Choose the provider and the setup</strong>, and test call quality with a few users first.</li>
<li><strong>Schedule the porting date</strong> with both providers, ideally early in the week, so problems are not left over a weekend.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>IP telephony brings flexible numbers, apps and lower costs, and it is replacing the classic phone line whether a business plans for it or not. Give calls a stable network with low latency, switch off SIP ALG, keep the network corner on a UPS and check emergency addresses and special devices before the switch.
