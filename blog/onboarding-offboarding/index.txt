15:T1117,<p>A new colleague starts on Monday and spends the first morning waiting for a laptop and a password. Two years later the same colleague leaves, and six months after that their account still has access to the shared drive, the shop backend and the company's social media. Both happen in almost every small business, and both are avoidable.</p>
<p>The good news: two short checklists, kept up to date, solve most of it.</p>
<h2 id="why-it-matters">Why It Matters</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Former employees with active access</strong> are a security risk, even with the best intentions: their accounts are rarely watched and often have old, reused passwords.</li>
<li><strong>Licences cost money.</strong> Forgotten accounts keep running in every cloud service billed per user.</li>
<li><strong>Knowledge disappears.</strong> Without a handover, nobody knows which supplier portal uses which login.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="the-foundation-one-account-to-rule-them-all">The Foundation: One Account to Rule Them All</h2>
<p>The more services use <strong>one company account</strong> for login, through single sign‑on with Microsoft, Google or a similar provider, the easier both days become. Switching off that one account then closes the door to every connected service at once. Everything that cannot use single sign‑on belongs on a list, because it needs to be handled by hand.</p>
<p>Keep that list, in a shared document or the business password manager, with every service, who has access and who is responsible.</p>
<h2 id="day-one-the-joiner-checklist">Day One: The Joiner Checklist</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Create the company account</strong> with two‑factor authentication from the start (<a href="/blog/passwords-passkeys-2fa/">why it matters</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Assign groups, not individual rights.</strong> "Accounting" or "Sales" grants the right folders and tools; nobody collects permissions one by one.</li>
<li><strong>Prepare the device</strong>: updated, encrypted, enrolled in device management if you use it, with the standard software installed.</li>
<li><strong>Hand over access to the password manager</strong>, shared vaults for the team only.</li>
<li><strong>Explain the basics in half an hour</strong>: where files belong, <a href="/blog/backups-3-2-1/">how backups work</a>, who to call if something looks suspicious. Knowing <a href="/blog/phishing-first-60-minutes/">what to do after a wrong click</a> belongs in that half hour.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="the-last-day-the-leaver-checklist">The Last Day: The Leaver Checklist</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Disable the company account</strong> at the agreed time, not weeks later. Disabling first, deleting later keeps data recoverable.</li>
<li><strong>Sign out all sessions and remove registered devices and passkeys</strong>, so existing logins on phones and laptops stop working too.</li>
<li><strong>Go through the list of services without single sign‑on</strong> and remove or change access there.</li>
<li><strong>Change shared passwords</strong> the person knew: Wi‑Fi, alarm codes, shared admin logins.</li>
<li><strong>Collect devices</strong> and wipe them, or remove company data remotely from personal phones.</li>
<li><strong>Handle the mailbox</strong>: an automatic reply pointing to a colleague is the privacy‑friendly default; check with your data protection guidance before forwarding personal mailboxes.</li>
<li><strong>Transfer ownership</strong> of files, shared folders and calendar entries before the account is deleted.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Worth knowing:</strong> Do a quick check every few months: compare the list of active accounts in each service with the list of current staff. Forgotten accounts show up immediately.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2 id="changing-roles">Changing Roles</h2>
<p>People also move inside the company. When someone switches from sales to accounting, remove the old group as well as adding the new one. Otherwise rights pile up, and after a few years an intern has access to everything.</p>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>Access in a small business should be granted on purpose and removed on schedule. Central accounts with single sign‑on make both easy, a list covers everything else, and two short checklists turn joining and leaving into routine.
