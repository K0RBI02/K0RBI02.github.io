15:T119d,<p>Monday morning: the printer shows up as "offline" on half the laptops. On Tuesday it works again. On Wednesday the scanner refuses to save to the shared folder that has worked for years. Printers have a reputation for being moody, but in a network they are usually not mysterious at all.</p>
<p>The good news: a handful of settings fix the vast majority of printer and scanner problems for good.</p>
<h2 id="why-printers-disappear">Why Printers "Disappear"</h2>
<h3 id="the-address-keeps-changing">The Address Keeps Changing</h3>
<p>Like every device, a printer gets its network address from the router. After a power cut or a few days off, it may get a different one, and computers that remember the old address can no longer find it.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>Quick fix:</strong> Give the printer a <strong>fixed address</strong> with a DHCP reservation in the router. The printer keeps its address, everything else stays automatic. <a href="/blog/ip-addresses-dhcp-nat/">How DHCP hands out addresses</a> explains the background.</p>
</blockquote>
<h3 id="wifi-naps">Wi‑Fi Naps</h3>
<p>Many printers put their Wi‑Fi to sleep to save power and wake up too slowly when a job arrives. If the printer is near a network socket, <strong>use a cable</strong>. It solves this problem and most others.</p>
<h3 id="discovery-across-networks">Discovery Across Networks</h3>
<p>Modern printers announce themselves on the network (via <em>mDNS</em>/Bonjour), which is how laptops and phones find them without drivers. This announcement does not cross from one network to another. If guests, staff and printers sit in <a href="/blog/segmenting-your-home-network/">separate networks</a>, the router needs an <a href="/blog/vlans-managed-switches/">mDNS forwarding feature</a>, or the printer has to be added by its address.</p>
<h2 id="printing-without-drivers">Printing Without Drivers</h2>
<p>Most printers sold in recent years support <strong>driverless printing</strong>: <em>IPP Everywhere</em>, Apple's <em>AirPrint</em> and <em>Mopria</em> on Android. Windows, macOS, iOS, Android and Linux can print to them without installing anything from the manufacturer. If a manufacturer's driver package causes trouble, removing it and adding the printer as a standard IPP printer often works better.</p>
<h2 id="why-scantofolder-broke">Why Scan‑to‑Folder Broke</h2>
<p>Scanning directly into a shared folder relies on the Windows file sharing protocol, SMB. Many older multifunction printers only speak its first version, <strong>SMB1</strong>, which is insecure and is no longer installed on current Windows versions. When the PC or the NAS was updated, scanning stopped.</p>
<p>The options, from best to worst:</p>
<div class="table-wrap"><table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Option</th>
<th>Verdict</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Firmware update that adds SMB2/SMB3</td>
<td>Best: check the manufacturer's site first</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Scan to a NAS that supports modern SMB</td>
<td>Good, and the files are backed up with the NAS</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Scan to email or to a cloud service</td>
<td>Works, but email providers are phasing out simple password logins for devices, so check what your printer supports</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Re‑enabling SMB1 on a computer</td>
<td>Avoid: it reopens a protocol that famous ransomware outbreaks used</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table></div>
<h2 id="printers-are-computers-too">Printers Are Computers Too</h2>
<p>A modern printer has a web interface, a network stack and sometimes stored copies of documents. Treat it accordingly:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Change the admin password</strong> of the web interface.</li>
<li><strong>Update the firmware.</strong> Printer updates fix security problems as well as scanning bugs.</li>
<li><strong>Never make a printer reachable from the internet.</strong> Search engines for exposed devices find thousands of them. The <a href="/blog/router-hardening/">router checklist</a> helps you find forgotten open ports.</li>
<li><strong>Switch off what you do not use</strong>, such as old protocols and cloud features.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="conclusion">Conclusion</h2>
<p>Most printer trouble comes down to changing addresses, sleepy Wi‑Fi, discovery across separated networks and old protocols. Give the printer a fixed address and a cable, use driverless printing, update the firmware and replace SMB1 scanning with something modern.
